# 39 ideas, scored and ranked

Four research agents each took one lens (civic data, money, history/culture, transit/tech) and wrote 9–10 ideas with sources and self-scores. The full write-ups are in [ideas/](ideas/). I then reviewed every idea, corrected eleven scores where a researcher was too generous, and re-ranked with weights that match your stated priorities.

## How the weighting works

Each idea is scored 1–5 on seven criteria. The weighted score (out of 100) uses:

| Criterion | Weight | Why |
|---|---:|---|
| Usefulness | 1.5 | Nobody returns to a site that doesn't help them |
| Low-upkeep | 1.5 | You have 1–3 hours a week and don't want to moderate |
| Uniqueness | 1.25 | A "great idea" fills a gap the BIDs and Petworth News leave |
| Monetization | 1.25 | Some income is one of your three goals |
| Traffic | 1.25 | Search and sharing potential |
| Build ease | 1.0 | Matters, but a hard build pays off if upkeep stays low |
| Skill fit | 0.5 | Almost every idea fits Astro + Cloudflare + Python, so it barely separates them |

The "Engine" column groups ideas by the shared code they run on. Ideas on the same engine reuse most of each other's build.

## What the ranking says

**1. One data engine powers about half of the ideas.** 17 of 39 ideas, including 9 of the top 12, are different views of the same nightly job: pull DC open data, clip it to a neighborhood polygon, strip personal fields, write JSON. Block Watch, the Data Digest, the 311 Report Card, Address Watch, the Assessment Check, the Realtor page, Curb Radar, the Liquor Pipeline and the Block Report Card are all features of one product, not separate projects. That is the strongest argument for building one platform rather than five unrelated sites. It's also why v1 is the engine plus its simplest view.

**2. The top two ideas are the same thing in two formats.** Block Watch (a weekly page) and the Data Digest (a weekly email with sponsors) differ only in delivery. Together they score highest, and they map onto the one revenue model with a documented solo comparable: a few exclusive local sponsors on an automated newsletter.

**3. History is the second engine and the best long-term SEO bet.** House & Block History Pages tie for first. They pair naturally with the Block Report Card: one page per address or block that shows what's happening now (permits, 311) next to what the place used to be (build year, architect, 1920s fire-insurance map). No competitor has both, and address pages keep attracting search traffic for years.

**4. L'Enfant Plaza needs a different product.** It has few residents, so the data engine has little to say there. The Transfer Coach (exits, escalators, live arrivals) is the best idea for that domain and the only one with real tourist traffic. Its upkeep is higher than the researcher scored, because someone has to re-walk the station when construction changes the routes.

**5. Drop these:**
- Hourly Jobs Board and Lost Restaurants Wall need moderation, which you said you don't want.
- Ask L'Enfant (AI concierge) costs money for every answer, and a made-up museum hour or exit hurts a visitor.
- Crowd Forecast is guesswork without ridership ground truth.
- Step-Free Status already exists as elevatoruptime.com.
