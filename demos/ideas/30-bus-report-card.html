How the grades are computed

  1. Collect. Every 30-60 seconds a cron Worker reads WMATA's bus GTFS-RT trip-updates feed (bus-gtfsrt-tripupdates.pb) and keeps, for each trip and stop, the latest predicted or actual time once the bus has passed. GTFS static gives the schedule.
  2. Headways. For each stop and route, sort observed arrivals and take the gap between consecutive buses. Compare it with the scheduled gap for that time of day.
  3. Gaps on schedule = share of observed gaps no longer than 1.5× the scheduled gap. Gaps over 2× = share longer than twice the schedule (the long waits riders remember). Bunched = share under a quarter of the schedule.
  4. On time = share of timepoint departures within a published window around the schedule (for example 2 minutes early to 7 minutes late). We would state the window on the page and keep it fixed.
  5. Ghost trips = scheduled trips that never appear in the real-time feed that day.
  6. Grade = gaps-on-schedule share, minus the gaps-over-2× share, minus 0.3 per ghost trip per 1,000 trips. A ≥ 75, B ≥ 65, C ≥ 55, D ≥ 45, else F. The formula is published so anyone can argue with it.

Only aggregate monthly statistics are published. WMATA's terms bar re-exposing its raw feed as an API, so there is no raw-data download.

About this demo

The idea
Monthly, automatically published reliability grades for the bus lines that serve each neighborhood, computed from WMATA's real-time GTFS-RT feed, with a plain-English summary and a chart residents can take to their ANC.
What's real here
The method. The GTFS-RT trip-updates feed it relies on is documented by WMATA. On 2026-10-31 the generator confirmed that DC GIS's old Metro bus-stop layer is marked retired as of June 2025, when the Better Bus network renamed routes and stops, which is why no route names appear.
What's mocked
Every grade, percentage, ghost-trip count, trip count, trend line, summary and the headway chart is sample data. The lines are described by corridor only ("Sample route A") and do not correspond to real routes. Real figures need weeks of GTFS-RT snapshots and a WMATA API key we don't have.
To build it for real
Get a WMATA key; run a cron Worker that stores trip-update snapshots in D1 or R2; refresh GTFS static weekly so post-Better-Bus route names stay right; a monthly GitHub Action runs the Python analysis, commits the grades and chart images, and rebuilds the page. Test early whether the GTFS-RT 1.0 feed carries the fields headway analysis needs, and share the method with WMATA before the first publish.
