Stop 1

L'Enfant Plaza

Start at the 7th & D St SW Metro exit

You're standing on the first urban-renewal project in Washington.

  • L'Enfant Plaza was built as part of the Southwest urban renewal project, the first in D.C., and was dedicated in 1968. Wikipedia: L’Enfant Plaza
  • In 1954 Southwest had about 3,900 buildings housing 4,500 families. About 60 percent of residents were African American, and only 20 percent owned their homes. Wikipedia
  • The ground under the plaza was mostly Victorian townhouses, plus a shuttered slaughterhouse. Wikipedia
  • The Southwest BID puts the toll at about 23,000 residents and 1,500 businesses displaced. SW BID history

The store that went to the Supreme Court

4th & G St SW (approximate: the old street grid is gone)

A department store on Fourth Street, taken for a plan it wasn't part of.

  • A department store stood at 712 Fourth Street SW. It wasn't blighted, but it sat in Project Area B, the first project under the 1946 D.C. Redevelopment Act, and was slated for clearance. Wikipedia: Berman v. Parker
  • The owner's estate sued and lost, 8–0, in Berman v. Parker (1954). The Court held that private property could be taken for a public purpose with just compensation, a ruling that laid the groundwork for Kelo v. City of New London (2005). Wikipedia
  • The planners' 1950 survey of the area reported 57.8 percent of dwellings with outside toilets and 29.6 percent without electricity. Wikipedia

Wheat Row

1315–1321 4th St SW

Four rowhouses from 1794 that renewal kept.

  • Begun in 1794 and finished in 1795, these are some of the oldest homes in D.C. and the city's first row houses. Wikipedia: Wheat Row
  • They were folded into the new Harbour Square cooperative in 1963 and listed on the National Register in 1973. Wikipedia
  • The Duncanson-Cranch House next door went up at about the same time, by the same speculators. Wikipedia

Thomas Law House

Near 6th & N St SW, inside Tiber Island

The "Honeymoon House" of Martha Washington's granddaughter.

  • Built 1794–96. Thomas Law moved in in March 1796 after marrying Eliza Parke Custis, Martha Washington's eldest granddaughter. Wikipedia: Thomas Law House
  • It was the Mt. Vernon Hotel during the Civil War and a medical clinic from 1923 to 1961. Wikipedia

Stop 5

Arena Stage

1101 6th St SW

A theater that chose the new Southwest in 1960.

  • Founded in 1950, Arena was the first racially integrated theater in Washington. Wikipedia: Arena Stage
  • It moved into this Harry Weese-designed home on Sixth Street in 1960; the glass-walled Mead Center renovation came in 2008–2010. Wikipedia

Stop 6

Maine Avenue Fish Market

1100 Maine Ave SW

Older than anything else on this walk except Wheat Row and the Law House.

  • Open since 1805, it is the oldest continuously operating fish market in the United States. Wikipedia: Maine Avenue Fish Market
  • It moved a few blocks along the Washington Channel in the 1960s, and still sells from floating barges. Wikipedia

Where did everyone go?

The 1946 Redevelopment Act let a new land agency take property by eminent domain and hand it to private developers. Wikipedia WETA's Boundary Stones says the project wiped out most of Southwest's buildings and many predominantly Black neighborhoods. WETA Families were moved out with only a fraction of their homes' value in compensation. Wikipedia

About this demo

The idea
A phone-first, grant-fundable walking tour for the people passing through L'Enfant Plaza: tourists, federal workers and Metro transfers. It tells the story of the Southwest neighborhood that renewal razed, stop by stop, with public-domain photos.
What's real here
Fetched on 2026-10-31: stop locations (DC Address Points, Intersection Points and Historic Landmarks), the map's streets, water and neighborhood boundary (Open Data DC), and the Library of Congress photos, each embedded only after its record said "No known restrictions". Every fact in the stop text was written from the source linked beside it. Facts I couldn't source, such as when St. Dominic's Church was built, were left out.
