# Atlas

**Idea:** the front page works like a sheet from a city atlas. One drawn plate of Petworth is the spine, and every story that has a place carries a grid reference (A–F, 1–6) that points to it.

**Layout.** Desktop: a text column (news, coming up, curb work this week, bigger projects) runs beside a sticky map plate. Items and map marks highlight each other. On a phone the plate comes after news and events, so headlines come first and the map still reads at full width. Then come the numbers, a three-column ledger (homes, 311, trees), Then, representatives and email.

**The map** is drawn from the data. Boundary rings, permit points, and a street grid rebuilt from geocoded permit and 311 addresses, with avenues fitted through their points. Grant and Sherman Circles sit where the fitted avenues cross. Markets and curb permits are placed by hundred-block arithmetic. Crime is never mapped.

**Type and color.** Newsreader for headlines and reading, IBM Plex Sans Condensed for labels and data, Plex Mono for grid references. White paper, blue-black ink, a teal boundary ribbon. Each layer gets one map hue: vermilion for projects, green for markets, ochre for curb, sepia for 1926.

**Dropped or demoted.** Commissioner, architect and builder names, because of the no-names rule (the 1926 sentence is rebuilt from the numbers). Cancelled liquor licenses, because the dates are missing. Jobs are now a link. 311 points are an optional layer, off by default.
